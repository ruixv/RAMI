Radar-Anchored Metric Initialization
for Gaussian Splatting under Suboptimal Illumination

‡ Corresponding author

Abstract

3D Gaussian splatting (3DGS) significantly facilitates novel-view synthesis from mobile captures, yet its performance severely degrades under low-illumination conditions. Single-chip millimeter-wave radar can compensate for unreliable visual geometry by providing range measurements even in the dark; however, existing sensor-guided 3DGS methods often use it to supervise training, failing under its coarse angular resolution and sparse pixel associations. In light of this challenge, we propose a radar-anchored metric initialization strategy, dubbed RAMI, which utilizes coarse radar measurements to calibrate initial 3DGS geometry, leaving subsequent refinement to vision without persistent radar supervision. Specifically, relative depth from a frozen monocular foundation model is metric-aligned to raw radar returns via a per-scene scale-and-shift optimization for Gaussian initialization. Evaluated on our illumination-stratified RadarEyes-GS benchmark, RAMI improves PSNR by up to +3.9 dB over random initialization, achieves superior worst-case robustness among all tested initialization schemes, and approaches high-cost LiDAR point-cloud accuracy within 0.16 dB without accessing LiDAR data. These results demonstrate that geometric injection strategy matters more than sensor grade for low-light mobile synthesis, paving the way of commodity radar techniques in a wide range of mobile acquisition and reconstruction tasks.

TL;DR. Low illumination breaks the SfM points that initialize 3DGS. A single-chip mmWave radar is too coarse to supervise individual pixels, but its range measurements can calibrate the scale and shift of monocular relative depth. Injected once, at initialization, this coarse geometry improves PSNR over random initialization by +1.11 dB on average across 23 scenes and matches a LiDAR point-cloud reference without using any LiDAR data.

Method

RAMI pipeline on real data from a benchmark capture. No new losses, no trainable components, no change to the 3DGS optimizer, only the initialization.

Qualitative Comparison

Eight scenes (dark → bright) against recent pipelines. Each row shows the view that maximizes RAMI's margin over its strongest competitor, a disclosed and favorable selection. RAMI is best on seven of eight rows; the exception is Office-C, where the official-recipe 3DGS-MCMC converges. All 23 scenes are in the supplementary.
